# SystemAnalysisAndDesign
Working on the DriverPass project, I basically had to design a full system for a small driving lesson company that wanted to offer online practice tests, online classes, and an easy way for customers to schedule real driving lessons. DriverPass was the client, and they needed a cloud‑based system that worked for customers, staff, the owner, and even the DMV. I think I did really well at breaking everything down into normal, everyday tasks instead of drowning it in technical language. If I had to revise one part, I’d probably go back and tighten up some of the early requirement sections, just to make them a little cleaner and more organized. When I interpreted the user’s needs, I focused on what each group actually does in real life like customers scheduling lessons, staff entering notes, the owner managing packages and made sure the system supported those tasks directly. Considering the user’s needs is huge because if the system doesn’t match how people actually work, it becomes confusing and nobody wants to use it. When I design software, I try to start by understanding the people first, then build the system around their workflow. Going forward, I’d keep using strategies like breaking tasks into simple actions, mapping out who does what, and making diagrams that show how everything connects. It helps keep the design grounded in reality instead of just being a bunch of technical boxes and arrows.
